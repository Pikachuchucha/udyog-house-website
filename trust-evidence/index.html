A label without its record is not used here.

Evidence labels are useful only when the reader can see exactly what was checked, by whom, when, and with what limits. No label is applied to any manufacturer on this preview site, because no check has been performed on any real manufacturer.

The ladder, at a glance

  1. P0 — Supplier-declared: the shop says so.
  2. P1 — Document received: a named document arrived and was logged.
  3. P2-ID — Identity cross-checked: who, matched to a source.
  4. P2-DOC — Document cross-checked: named fields matched to a source.
  5. P3 — Observed capability: a named machine witnessed, measured as stated.
  6. P4 — Transaction-evidenced: one specific past transaction, by stated evidence.

Binding display rules

Five rules every label must obey

Five binding display rules, beside the ladder above
RuleWhat it requires
Rule 1 of 5 — One field, never a businessEvery label belongs to one named field. A label is never a badge for a profile, a business, or a person, and the highest label on one field says nothing about any other field.
Rule 2 of 5 — No label without its recordNo label is displayed unless scope, what was checked, method, checker (role and relationship to the supplier), source, date, and limitations are all filled in. A missing field means no label.
Rule 3 of 5 — The supplier is never its own checkerAbove P0, the checker is never the supplier itself. The checker's role and relationship to the supplier are stated beside the label.
Rule 4 of 5 — Edits and disputes downgrade, never hideAn owner edit to a labelled field drops that field to P0 until it is re-checked. A disputed field's label is suspended as “under review” (proposed default, to be confirmed before first publication) — never silently left standing.
Rule 5 of 5 — Payment never changes a labelPayment, if ever introduced, will never upgrade an evidence label, improve search position, or add any trust badge.

Full definitions

Every level, in full

Open each level for its meaning, what was checked, and its limits. P2 is shown split, because identity and documents are two different checks and must never share one label.

P0 — Supplier-declared

Meaning: the supplier’s own words about one named field of capability.

What was checked: nothing. No independent check is recorded for P0.

Checker: none. Source: the supplier’s statement, with its date.

Limits: a P0 statement does not prove capability, quality, capacity, reliability, or delivery. It is the starting point, and it stays the label for every draft record made with the tools on this site.

P1 — Document received

Meaning: a named document was received and logged, beside the field it relates to.

What was checked: receipt and logging only. The document type, its own date, and its validity or expiry are shown.

Relevance rule: a document counts only beside the field it relates to. Registration and identity documents are never capability evidence, and an expired document is shown as expired.

Limits: receipt is not verification, authenticity, or currency. A received document alone does not prove capability or delivery.

P2-ID — Identity cross-checked

Meaning: identity, or the authorised representative, matched to a named independent source, on a named date.

What was checked: identity fields only — nothing about what the business can make.

Limits: P2-ID establishes nothing about capability, quality, capacity, reliability, or delivery. It answers “who is this?” and nothing else.

P2-DOC — Document cross-checked

Meaning: named document fields matched to a named independent source, on a named date.

What was checked: only the named fields, against only the named source. The document, its fields, and the source are all stated.

Limits: matching a document does not prove the shop can do the work, can repeat it, or will deliver it. P2-DOC is about documents, not capability.

P3 — Observed capability

Meaning: a named machine was witnessed producing comparable work in a named material, measured by a stated method to a stated tolerance.

What was checked: production and measurement, witnessed together, on a named date, by a stated checker (role and relationship to the supplier).

Exclusions: seeing equipment, a facility, a photograph, a brochure, or a finished part — without witnessing production and measurement — does not earn P3.

Limits: point in time only. Observation does not prove ongoing quality, capacity, or delivery, and the observer is not an accredited inspector.

P4 — Transaction-evidenced

Meaning: one specific past transaction, evidenced by a stated evidence type, with transaction date and evidence date both mandatory.

Evidence ranked: counterparty-confirmed evidence is strongest; supplier-held documents are weakest and are labelled as such. What was delivered is stated; acceptance and timeliness are shown as evidenced or unknown, never assumed.

Limits: one past transaction only. P4 does not establish an inspection pass, timeliness, satisfaction, or repeatability, and it is not a rating, a testimonial, or a future-performance guarantee.

Proposed, not decided — kept apart from the definitions on purpose. Recheck intervals (P2 and P3 annual) are proposals, not decisions. Marking P4 evidence as “historical (date)” after 12 months unless refreshed is a proposal, not a decision. The disputed-field default (“under review” suspension) is proposed, to be confirmed before first publication. None of these is policy yet, and none is applied to anyone.

What is never claimed

No blanket claim. No shortcut to trust.

  • No blanket claim is made that a manufacturer is “verified”, certified, accredited, approved, or vetted in general terms — and none is planned. Such words appear only inside a specific field record, with scope, checker, date, and limits.
  • Udyog House is not an accredited inspection or certification body.
  • A factory visit, an identity document, or a registration document alone does not prove capability, quality, capacity, reliability, or delivery.
  • No pay-to-rank, pay-to-verify, or opaque trust score is offered or implied.
  • No ranking, testimonial, customer logo, partnership, transaction, or revenue claim is made on this site.
  • Expired or unrechecked labels must be downgraded or removed — silence is not an option, once labels exist. No label exists yet.

Who checks?

The checker for a field is named by role and relationship to the supplier, beside the label. The checker is never the supplier above P0, and is not an accredited inspector. The role and competence criteria for checkers are still to be defined before any check is performed — that definition is a launch item, not a preview claim.

Correction and removal — a gate, not an afterthought

A correction and removal route for published manufacturer information must be live before any profile is ever published. That route is not live here, because no profile is published here. Its design and response window are still to be staffed and confirmed. The full joining method, including owner approval per field, is on For Manufacturers.
