Evidence to request — and what each level does not prove

  • P0 Supplier-declared: the shop's own words. A starting point; not a check.
  • P1 Document received: a document was received and logged. Receipt is not authenticity, currency, or capability.
  • P2-ID Identity cross-checked: identity / authorised representative only. Proves nothing about capability.
  • P2-DOC Document cross-checked: named document fields matched to a named source. About documents, not capability.
  • P3 Observed capability: a named machine witnessed producing comparable work, measured as stated. Point in time only.
  • P4 Transaction-evidenced: only that specific past transaction, by stated evidence. Counterparty-confirmed is strongest; supplier-held documents are weakest. Not a rating or a future guarantee.
  • Name the grade, not just the material family: a quote in “tool steel” or “stainless steel” alone cannot be compared. Ask which grade, in what condition, and whether a mill certificate is available.

A short guide — not a new service

How to brief a tool room or mould maker before sharing a drawing

Work through these in order. Each one prevents a different kind of wasted conversation.

  1. 1

    Process

    Say what kind of work it is — CNC milling, turning, grinding, mould making, mould-base machining — and whether it is new work, a repair, a modification, or maintenance. A shop that makes new moulds may not be the shop that repairs one well.

  2. 2

    Material

    Name the grade and condition, not just the family: SS 316 rather than “stainless steel”, P20 rather than “tool steel”. Ask who supplies the material and whether a mill certificate is available. The grade is the quote.

  3. 3

    Tolerance band

    Name the band, the feature it applies to, the size, the finish (Ra), and how it will be measured. A tolerance without a feature, a size, and an instrument cannot be checked — and a fine tolerance on a declared claim alone should not be accepted.

  4. 4

    Quantity band

    Say whether this is a single piece, a small batch, or repeat work. Quantity changes what matters: for larger or repeat runs, ask about batch drift, acceptance criteria, and who bears rework and scrap cost.

  5. 5

    Evidence to request

    Ask for the evidence behind the specific field, not a general assurance. What each label means — and what it does not prove — is on Trust & Evidence.

When you do share a drawing

A complete request-for-quotation pack, when the time comes, is: a 2D drawing with dimensions and tolerances, a 3D model where one exists, the grade, the quantity, the finish, the inspection requirement, and the delivery need. Share it only through a direct, agreed channel with a named shop, once the scope is clear — never through a preview form. Confidentiality terms for that channel are agreed directly between buyer and shop; this preview site sets none, because it carries no drawings at all.

Plain prose, no jargon assumed

Glossary: words shops use, in plain language

Tool room
A workshop that makes, repairs, and maintains tools, dies, moulds, jigs, and fixtures — usually one-off or small-batch precision work.
What to ask: what tool-room work do you do most — new, repair, modification, or maintenance? What evidence supports the tolerance you claim?
Mould making
Making the mould that shapes a product, most often by injecting or pressing material into a cavity.
What to ask: how many cavities, what runner, what expected shots and mould life, and what are the tryout and correction terms?
Mould-base manufacturing
Making the standard or custom base that holds a mould: plates, pillars, bushes, ejectors, and water lines, fitted together.
What to ask: is this raw plates or a fitted base, what pocketing and guide work is included, and is heat treatment included?
CNC machining
Computer Numerical Control machining: a computer-controlled machine cutting a part to a programmed shape.
What to ask: which machine, what travel, and what size part fits it? A machine name without travel is half an answer.
VMC machining
Machining on a vertical machining centre (VMC): a CNC milling machine whose spindle is vertical.
What to ask: 3-axis or 4/5-axis, and what envelope does the job need? A VMC is a kind of CNC milling machine, not a separate process.
Precision job-work
Work done on a customer’s order or material, to tight tolerances, usually in small batches.
What to ask: what tolerance have you actually held on comparable work, and what inspection record shows it?
Tolerance bands
Ranges of permitted deviation from a stated size, for example general (±0.1 mm and looser), precision (±0.02–0.05 mm), or fine (±0.01 mm or tighter).
What to ask: on which feature, at what size, with what finish, measured by what instrument? A band without a feature cannot be checked.
Materials
The metal or plastic a part is made from — named properly, that means a grade (SS 316, P20, EN24, AA 6061), in a stated condition, with a certificate answer.
What to ask: which grade, in what condition, who supplies it, and is a mill certificate available?

Questions, answered plainly

Questions buyers actually ask

How do I find a tool room that can hold my tolerance?

Start from the tolerance with its feature, size, finish, and instrument — then ask what the shop has actually held on comparable work, and what record shows it. A declared claim alone is the weakest evidence, especially at fine tolerances.

What should I ask before sending a drawing?

Process, machine and travel, material grade and condition, tolerance band, quantity band, and the evidence you want to see for that specific field. The checker above turns those answers into a question sheet you can copy and print.

What does an evidence label actually guarantee?

Nothing about the future. Each label describes one past check on one field, with its limits. The full definitions are on Trust & Evidence.

Can Udyog House recommend a shop for my job?

Not on this preview site, and not as a general claim anywhere: no shop is listed, ranked, or recommended here. The honest step today is a better brief — and, when records exist, a record you can read for yourself.
